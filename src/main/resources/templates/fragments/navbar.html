Deep-Space Communication Modelling Using Poisson & Geometric Distributions

1. Project Overview & Research Motivation

Signals travelling between deep-space probes and Earth ground stations traverse vast interplanetary distances plagued by solar interference, cosmic noise, signal attenuation, and atmospheric disruption. Because packet drops and delayed link lock are inherently uncertain, discrete probability models provide a rigorous mathematical framework for quantifying communication reliability.

2. Dual-Model Statistical Framework

Deep-space communication engineering requires answering two distinct statistical questions for every telemetry contact session:

Module 1 · Poisson Distribution

Quantifying Transmission Drops

By treating packet drops as discrete random events across a fixed observation window, the Poisson distribution models expected disruption density λ and reveals the likelihood of zero-drop versus high-loss communication intervals.

P(X = k) = (λk e−λ) / k!
Module 2 · Geometric Distribution

First Successful Transmission

When a spacecraft initiates repeated transmission attempts under noisy channel conditions, the Geometric distribution captures the waiting time in discrete trials until the first successful acknowledgement reaches Earth. Attempt 1 carries the highest single-attempt probability.

P(Y = k) = (1 − p)k − 1 p

3. Communication Reliability Analysis

Cumulative link reliability evaluates the overall probability of establishing successful communication within a finite horizon of n attempts:

R(n) = P(Y ≤ n) = 1 − (1 − p)n

The tail exceedance risk P(Y > n) = (1 − p)n quantifies the residual risk that link acquisition requires more than n attempts. Milestone analysis evaluates the attempt horizons required to achieve 90%, 95%, and 99% target reliability thresholds.

4. Defined Scope & Theoretical Assumptions

  • Stationary Poisson Rate (λ): Drops occur at a constant average rate over the evaluated communication window, assuming independent arrival increments.
  • Independent Bernoulli Trials (p): Each transmission attempt has a constant single-attempt success probability p with no channel memory across retries.
  • Strict Dual-Model Boundary: This analytical system intentionally uses only the Poisson distribution and the Geometric distribution, preserving mathematical clarity and direct alignment with academic research objectives.
